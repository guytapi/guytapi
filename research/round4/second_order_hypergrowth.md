# Round 4: Second-order consequences of AI hypergrowth (as of 2026-10-05)

**Method:** Find AI products or behaviors growing fast in 2026, then look for the pain they create for *other* businesses (the receivers, counterparties and downstream operators), not for the adopters. Every consequence was checked for evidence of pain and for competitor funding. I used 38 web searches; WebFetch was blocked on most sites, so the evidence comes from search-result snippets. **Verify key numbers at the source before using them in a deck.**
Legend: "(unverified)" means I recalled it or saw it only in a secondary source and did not confirm it. Each URL below appeared in a search result; none were made up.

**Patterns across the table:**
- **Fraud and verification problems caused by gen-AI are the most crowded area** (rows 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13). Incumbents or well-funded vertical leaders already own most of them.
- **"Volume shock" pain is the least served:** AI raises the number of inbound items a business must process (vuln reports, RFQs, calls, bids) faster than humans can triage them. White space shows up when the receiver is a business with budget (an ISV or a distributor), not an SMB or OSS maintainer.

## 2. Top 3 theses

### #1 PSIRT Autopilot: product-security response for software vendors
- **One-sentence pitch:** "AI now finds bugs in your product faster than your team can triage them. We take every inbound vulnerability report and scanner finding, reproduce it, rule out slop, write and backport the fix across supported versions, and publish the advisory and VEX within hours, not weeks."
- **Buyer:** Head of Product Security or VP Engineering at B2B software vendors (ISVs). Budget comes from security/eng. Not a regulated buyer.
- **Buyers × ACV:** About 5,000 to 8,000 software vendors with $20M+ ARR worldwide, plus device/OT vendors (estimate, unverified). 6,000 × $100K = **$600M SAM**. Expands with per-version and per-product pricing. Large vendors (Microsoft, Oracle, Cisco) pay $1M+.
- **Why now:**
  - Record patch volumes in July/Aug 2026, which vendors attribute to AI discovery.
  - Time-to-exploit has collapsed (~10h per CSA; -7 days per M-Trends).
  - NIST NVD gave up full enrichment.
  - curl and other bounties collapsed under AI slop.
  - Enterprise customers are adding sub-day SLAs (CERT-In 12h).
- **90-day pilot:** Connect the bounty inbox, GitHub/GitLab and the CI pipeline. Success metric: median time from report to validated fix and advisory on the vendor's real backlog.
- **Kill risks:**
  - HackerOne or Bugcrowd bundle "agentic validation and fix." HackerOne already announced validation.
  - It overlaps the killed autonomous-code-merge thesis. We must sell triage, reproduction, backport and advisory, with a human approving the merge.
  - GitHub (Copilot Autofix plus security advisories) could ship it natively.
  - Proving the reproduction step is accurate is hard.

### #2 Evidence authenticity for operations ("know your evidence")
- **One-sentence pitch:** "Gen-AI made every photo and document your counterparties send you forgeable. We verify damage photos, proofs of delivery, BOLs, COIs and new-vendor invoices at intake, inside the ops workflow, before you pay a refund, release a load or onboard a sub."
- **Buyer:** VP Operations, Loss Prevention or Risk at non-bank businesses. Wedge options are DTC/retail returns or 3PL and freight claims. Expansion goes to construction GCs and property managers (COIs) and B2B marketplaces. Avoid lenders and insurers (excluded).
- **Buyers × ACV:** About 4,000 US retailers and brands doing >$50M online, plus ~3,000 brokers/3PLs, plus ~3,000 large GCs and property managers, ≈ 10,000 buyers (estimate, unverified). 10,000 × $60K = **$600M SAM**, with usage-based upside per verification.
- **Why now:**
  - Inscribe: AI-generated fraud up 5x, 1 in 16 documents fraudulent.
  - Inpainted damage photos beat whole-image detectors (Forter's $1.5M ring).
  - Freight fraud is at record levels.
  - Existing tools sit in lending and KYC flows, not ops flows.
- **90-day pilot:** Shadow-mode scoring on all returns and claims, measuring fraud dollars caught versus false positives.
- **Kill risks:**
  - Forter, Riskified or Highway add it as a feature in their verticals.
  - Resistant AI or Inscribe move down-market into ops.
  - Detection is an arms race; C2PA and camera-attested capture could make detection a commodity. Mitigation: own capture-at-source plus cross-document consistency checks.
  - Horizontal positioning blurs the story, so pick one vertical wedge.

### #3 Inbound-demand triage for industrial suppliers in the AI-RFQ flood
- **One-sentence pitch:** "Buyers' AI tools now blast RFQs to dozens of suppliers at once. We qualify, dedupe and auto-quote every inbound RFQ and call, so a distributor's inside-sales team only works real, winnable demand and still answers first."
- **Buyer:** VP Sales or COO at mid-size industrial distributors and job-shop manufacturers. Not regulated.
- **Buyers × ACV:** About 10,000 US/EU distributors and manufacturers with $50M+ revenue (estimate, unverified). 10,000 × $50K = **$500M SAM**. Upside comes from per-quote or GMV-based pricing.
- **Why now:**
  - Procurement-side AI (RFQ generation, supplier shortlisting) raises inbound volume.
  - First credible quote wins (Instalily data).
  - Avent quotes about $200K per day for distributors.
  - Competitors are all seed stage (Mercura $2.1M, Avent $500K, Hexa $500K).
- **90-day pilot:** Plug into the shared RFQ inbox and the ERP. Measure response time, quote-to-win rate and human hours per quote.
- **Kill risks:**
  - **Evidence of the AI-RFQ flood is thin** (one blocked article). Today this may just be "quote automation," which is a crowded YC category with lots of seed entrants.
  - ACV for distributors may land at $20 to 40K, below the bar.
  - ERP vendors (Epicor, Infor, Prophet 21) could bundle it.
  - It sits close to the killed "agentic B2B commerce" thesis. The difference: agents send RFQs today; they don't place orders.
