Date: 2026-10-05. Method: start from real-economy events dated roughly May to Oct 2026, not from AI tooling. 40 web searches. Every URL below came back in a search result; I did not fetch the pages (WebFetch not used). **(U)** marks a claim or a competitor name that comes from my memory or from one low-quality source and was not confirmed by a search.

Bar: B2B, $1B+ potential, ACV $50K+, thousands of buyers, a 90-day pilot, understandable to a VC in one sentence, huge pain now. No government, defense, banking, insurance or regulated-healthcare buyers.

## Executive takeaways

1. **The strongest new pressure is the US move from tariff *rates* to tariff *enforcement*.** The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs (Feb 2026). Since then: Section 301 tariffs on 16 partners (Jul), Section 301 forced-labor findings (Jun 2), the White House "Great Transshipment Scam" report and its AI "Detective Border" tool (Aug 13), a record $549.5M False Claims Act customs settlement (May 12), and USMCA not renewed (Jul 1). Together these move the burden of proof onto importers: "prove where this was really made."
2. **Product-data obligations are piling onto the same mid-market manufacturers all at once.** Minnesota PFAS reports were due Sep 15. Oregon, Colorado and California packaging EPR fees ran Jul to Aug. EU PPWR started Aug 12. Actual CBAM emissions are needed before certificate purchases start Feb 2027. FEOC cost-ratio math (Feb 2026 notice). EUDR applies Dec 30. The EU Data Act design rule started Sep 12. Software for each single regulation is already crowded (Assent, osapiens, Coolset, CarbonChain, Packgine and others). What looks open is **the supplier side**: the companies that actually hold the data and get hit with every questionnaire.
3. **The Hormuz closure (from about Feb 28 2026, six or more months) is the largest cost shock.** Polyethylene prices nearly doubled, Far East to US West Coast container rates rose 192%, diesel hit a record $5.85, and urea topped $600. On top of tariff churn and IEEPA refunds, mid-market manufacturers and distributors cannot reprice fast enough.
4. **Several "obvious" events are negative or already crowded.** ACF repeal, the CSRD Omnibus (cuts scope by about 90%), pay transparency, SB 253, battery passports, EUDR and tariff-refund recovery.

## Top 3 ideas, fully argued

### Idea #1: Proof-of-Origin Network ("prove where it was made before CBP's AI asks")

**Pitch:** US trade policy moved from tariff rates to enforcement, with a 40% transshipment penalty, record False Claims Act payouts and AI border screening. We collect primary production evidence from your overseas factories and turn every shipment into a defensible origin file.

**What it does:** Suppliers in Vietnam, Thailand, Mexico and elsewhere connect their production data: bills of materials, sub-supplier invoices, production logs, energy and utility use, payroll headcount, and machine or line capacity. The AI cross-checks claimed output against physical capacity (the same test "Detective Border" runs), computes substantial transformation, regional value content and Section 232 metal-content positions, and produces a CF-28/CF-29, EAPA and FCA-defense dossier per entry.

**Exciting framing:** "The supply chain's proof layer." Every product crossing a border will need a verifiable production history. Today that history is a supplier affidavit.

**Buyer:** VP Trade Compliance or CFO at US importers sourcing from Asia outside China and from Mexico. Trade counsel are the channel, because they are flooded with clients after Perfectus.

**Buyers × ACV:** About 330K importers are owed IEEPA refunds. Perhaps 15–25K import more than $25M a year from affected corridors (U). At $60–250K ACV, about 8K paying would be $1–1.5B ARR potential. Supplier-side seats add network revenue.

**Why now:** Perfectus $549.5M (May 12); customs FCA recoveries above $570M in 5 months; Section 301 forced-labor findings (Jun 2); the transshipment report and Detective Border (Aug 13); Section 301 excess-capacity tariffs (Jul); USMCA annual reviews (Jul 1). The legal standard is "clear and convincing evidence," and supplier letters do not meet it.

**Why incumbents can't:** Brokers file entries; they do not audit factories. Altana is the real threat: about $323M raised, $1B valuation, a CBP contract, product passports, and a Maersk network (Apr 2026). But Altana is built from trade-data graphs and government-side screening. The wedge is *importer-side legal defense from primary factory evidence*. Altana sitting on the CBP side is also a conflict for importers preparing a defense.

**90-day pilot:** One importer with $100M+ Vietnam/Thailand sourcing. Onboard its top 10 suppliers. Produce origin dossiers for its top 50 SKUs. Run a mock CF-28 response and a capacity-anomaly scan. Success means 2+ risky SKUs found, and counsel signing off that the dossiers are defensible.

**Kill risks:** (1) Altana ships factory-evidence collection. (2) Suppliers refuse to share cost data, which is the classic adoption problem. (3) Enforcement intensity is political and could ease after a trade deal. (4) The buyer may see it as insurance against an unlikely event unless they have had a CF-28.

**Verdict: strongest "huge pain now" match. Competition risk: medium to high (Altana).**

### Idea #2: Supplier-side product data agent ("Vanta for product compliance data, owned by the supplier")

**Pitch:** Every mid-market manufacturer is now flooded with product-data questions from customers: PFAS (Minnesota Sep 15), EPR packaging (OR/CO/CA plus four more states), PPWR, CBAM actual emissions, FEOC cost ratios, EUDR reference numbers, origin affidavits, conflict minerals. Our AI agent builds one product-data graph from their ERP, BOMs, SDSs and test reports, and answers every customer portal and questionnaire automatically.

**Exciting framing:** "The supply chain data network." Every physical product will carry a digital passport by 2028 (ESPR, battery, Data Act). We are the system of record that the *data owners*, the suppliers, control and share by link. That is the Plaid model: a network across the 3–5 buyer-side portals each supplier is forced into.

**Proven analog:** Security questionnaires went the same way. Buyer-side GRC tools (OneTrust and others) were followed by supplier-side trust centers and AI answering tools (Vanta Trust Center, SecurityPal, Conveyor; U on current funding). Product compliance is earlier on that curve. The incumbents, Assent (now with IPOINT), osapiens, Coolset and Packgine, are buyer-paid portals that each ask the supplier to re-enter the same data. A search found no supplier-side "answer once" player.

**Buyer:** VP Quality / Regulatory or a product compliance manager at component and material suppliers with $50M–$2B revenue: plastics, chemicals, electronics components, packaging converters, metal fabrication. They already pay compliance consultants and staff to fill in portals.

**Buyers × ACV:** US manufacturers with 500+ employees number about 4–5K, and with 100–499 employees about 15K+ (U, Census). EU Mittelstand plus Asian exporters into the EU add a similar number. At $50–120K ACV, about 10K buyers would be about $1B ARR potential. Buyer-side companies could also pay for "verified supplier data" feeds.

**Why now:** At least six obligations hit the same supplier inside 12 months (May to Dec 2026), plus the CBAM certificate cash cost in Feb 2027, which makes *actual* emissions worth money compared with default values. The CSRD value-chain cap even gives suppliers a right to refuse requests that are excessive, so the supplier becomes the party that controls the data.

**Why incumbents can't:** Assent's business is buyers paying to chase suppliers. Making suppliers' data portable across competing portals cannibalizes its network lock-in. LLMs make supplier-side extraction from messy documents newly cheap.

**90-day pilot:** One $300M plastics or components supplier with more than 200 open customer requests. Ingest ERP, BOM and SDS data. Auto-answer Minnesota PFAS, EPR packaging specs and 3 customer portals. Metric: more than 70% of answers accepted with no edits, and hours saved against the consultant budget.

**Kill risks:** (1) Suppliers see compliance as a cost center and may not pay $50K+. The mitigation is pricing against the consultant and staff spend it replaces. (2) Assent offers a free supplier-side profile. (3) Regulations keep slipping (TSCA, EUDR, DPP), which dulls urgency. (4) Portals may block automated submission.

**Verdict: best "where the world is going" framing and the least crowded on the supplier side. Willingness to pay at $50K+ is unproven.**

### Idea #3: Autonomous margin defense ("reprice every contract the day input costs move")

**Pitch:** In 2026, mid-market manufacturers and distributors absorbed resin prices up about 100%, diesel up 58%, container rates up 192%, three changes of tariff regime (IEEPA, then Section 122/232, then Section 301), and refund reversals. We are an AI agent that reads every customer contract and cost input and executes surcharges, escalators and price changes automatically.

**What it does:** It ingests customer contracts (escalation clauses, index links, notice periods), supplier price notices, tariff changes and commodity indices. It computes margin leakage per customer and SKU, drafts the price-change notices and surcharge invoices that the contract permits, and tracks whether customers accept.

**Exciting framing:** "Pricing becomes real-time for the physical economy." Volatility is the new normal (Hormuz, tariffs, power prices).

**Buyer:** CFO or VP Pricing at manufacturers and distributors with $100M–$3B revenue: plastics converters, packaging, industrial distribution, food ingredients.

**Buyers × ACV:** About 15–20K US companies in this band (U). At $60–150K ACV, about 8K would be $1B+ potential. Gainshare pricing on recovered margin is also an option.

**Why now:** The Hormuz cost shock (Mar to Sep 2026); MSC and Grainger say supplier price pressure is "worse than post-COVID"; the Fed is on hold with no cuts; Section 301 replacement tariffs (Jul 2026); refund pass-through disputes.

**Why incumbents can't:** PROS, Zilliant, Vendavo and Pricefx are 12-month enterprise list-price optimization deployments. They do not read contracts or execute clause-by-clause pass-through. ERPs (Epicor, Infor) hold the prices but no intelligence.

**90-day pilot:** One $500M plastics converter. Load its top 100 customer contracts and resin/freight indices. Find recoverable margin it is allowed to recover but has not, and send notices. Target: recover at least 1% of revenue.

**Kill risks:** (1) This is the "contract-to-cash" cluster the founder rated dull, and the framing may not save it. (2) If the shock fades in 2027, so does the urgency. (3) Pricing incumbents add contract-reading agents. (4) Sales have to tolerate customer friction.

**Verdict: biggest immediate dollar pain. Weakest on the "interesting" filter. Competition not deeply searched (U).**
