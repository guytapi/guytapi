# Round 6: Fresh tech events (Jun–Oct 2026) that create new B2B needs

Date: 2026-10-05. Method: FRESHNESS. Look for events from the last ~4 months where startups haven't had time to form yet. 39 web searches.
**Verification:** every URL below appeared in a search result. I did not fetch the pages (WebFetch was not used), so facts come from search snippets. **[UNVERIFIED]** marks claims I could not confirm through search. I did not invent any URLs.

## Key pattern in the evidence
Half of the "fresh" events are **kill signals rather than openings**: platforms absorbing layers (Okta Agent SSO free, Workday Agent SoR, Stripe→OpenRouter, PANW→Portkey, Salesforce→Fin, OpenAI Deployment Co, Colorado AI Act repealed, SOX 404(b) shrinking). The real openings come from **toll/permission changes that put enterprises in conflict with their own vendors**. Platforms cannot be neutral on these because they are the counterparty. Examples: SaaS vendors metering or banning third-party agents, and AI ad surfaces that each want brands on their own rails.

## Top 3 ideas

### TOP 1: Agent Toll Router ("route around the agent tax")
**Pitch:** "Every SaaS vendor now charges your AI agents per action and restricts how they connect. We route each agent call down the cheapest path the contract allows: reads come from your own warehouse copy, and only writes go through the vendor's metered gateway."

- **Events:** ServiceNow Action Fabric (May), SAP API v4/2026 enforcement (Jun 9), Salesforce/HubSpot/Microsoft/Workday/Snowflake per-action pricing (within ~one quarter), Gartner's $234B "agentic arbitrage" warning (Jul 1), Claude/ChatGPT/Copilot Autopilot agents rolling out at enterprise scale (Sep).
- **Buyer:** CIO plus Head of Enterprise Apps/Architecture; co-sponsor is the FinOps/procurement lead at renewal.
- **Buyers × ACV:** ~6,000 enterprises with 30+ strategic SaaS apps and agent programs × $150K ≈ $0.9B ARR serviceable. Priced as a share of avoided tolls, so it scales with agent volume. Path to $1B+ if agents grow as forecast.
- **Product:** (1) inventory of agent→SaaS calls (MCP/A2A/API) from gateways and logs; (2) a contract and policy graph: per vendor, what agent access is permitted (SAP: ODP over OData OK, RFC banned), the unit price and the cap; (3) a read-path compiler that answers agent reads from data already replicated to Snowflake/Databricks (Fivetran etc.) when the license allows; (4) renewal pack with actual agent-action volumes for negotiating agent-access clauses.
- **Why now:** these toll mechanisms are 1–5 months old and published per-action rates appeared in 2026. 2026 renewals lock in five-year baselines (per Gartner guidance). No dedicated startup surfaced in search; Finout only has a blog post.
- **Why incumbents can't:** SaaS vendors are the toll collectors. MuleSoft is owned by Salesforce, and SAP Integration Suite is SAP. Snowflake/Databricks are themselves metering agents (Snowflake is in the list). PANW/Portkey is a security gateway with no contract or data-path logic. SaaS-spend tools (Zylo, Tropic, Vertice) see invoices, not agent call paths.
- **90-day pilot:** connect to one company's agent gateway plus ServiceNow and Salesforce. Baseline the projected annual agent-action fees. Reroute read calls to the existing warehouse copy. Target a ≥40% cut in metered actions on two workflows, plus a clause-gap report for the next renewal.
- **Kill risks:** (a) vendors rewrite terms to forbid agents from using replicated data (SAP already restricts replication outside BDC); this is the biggest risk. (b) Per-action prices drop or get bundled into enterprise agreements, which shrinks savings. (c) Zylo/Tropic or Finout add an "agent" module quickly. (d) It sounds like the already-killed "AI spend control". The pitch must stay about routing and contract rights, not dashboards.
- **Verdict:** best fit to the FRESHNESS method (enterprise vs vendor conflict, under 6 months old). Next step: 10 calls with enterprise-apps leaders on ServiceNow and SAP to confirm actual fees being billed today.

### TOP 2: Brand Agent OS for AI ad and assistant surfaces
**Pitch:** "ChatGPT, Meta, Siri and Gemini now let brands put a selling agent inside the assistant. We run one governed brand agent across all of them and prove which conversations turned into revenue."

- **Events:** ChatGPT Sponsored Agents plus HubSpot/Shopify ads (Sep 16/23), Meta Business Agent global (Aug), Apple App Intents-only Siri with third-party AI hand-off (WWDC Jun; iOS 27 fall), Amazon Seller Central agent plugin (Sep 23), Ninth Circuit Comet ruling (Aug; brands can't simply block agents), EU AI Act Art. 50 deployer disclosure (Aug 2).
- **Buyer:** VP E-commerce / CMO at mid-to-large consumer brands and retailers (Best Buy, Lowe's, Newegg and VistaPrint are named early Sponsored Agent advertisers).
- **Buyers × ACV:** ~8,000 brands with >$50M online revenue (US+EU) **[estimate]** × $100K ≈ $0.8B. Upside comes from a performance take-rate on agent-attributed revenue.
- **Product:** one brand "truth layer" (catalog, live price/inventory, policies, claims compliance, Art. 50 disclosure) compiled into each surface's format (Sponsored Agent, Meta Business Agent Platform, App Intents, Claude connector). Includes conversation QA (no off-policy promises or discounts), cross-surface attribution, and A/B testing of agent behavior.
- **Why now:** Sponsored Agents is 3 weeks old and in limited US test. Each surface launched separately within ~90 days, so fragmentation is brand new.
- **Why incumbents can't:** each platform optimizes for its own surface (OpenAI, Meta and Apple won't build for each other). Shopify serves SMB and its own checkout. Salesforce Agentforce Commerce is tied to SFCC.
- **90-day pilot:** one retailer in the Sponsored Agents test. Deploy the same brand agent to ChatGPT plus WhatsApp. Measure conversion vs. a landing page and policy-violation rate. Success = lower CAC on agent-ad traffic with zero compliance incidents.
- **Kill risks:** (a) **Sierra** (and Decagon) already build consumer-brand agents and can extend to ad surfaces; this is the strongest threat. (b) OpenAI and Meta give builders away free. (c) Sponsored Agents may stay a small test (like the OpenAI app store, which underperformed). (d) It sits close to the killed GEO and AI-support-agent clusters.
- **Verdict:** high why-now, real Sierra risk. Next step: check Sierra/Decagon announcements for ad surfaces and talk to 5 Sponsored-Agent test advertisers.

### TOP 3: AI Continuity: real failover for AI in the revenue path
**Pitch:** "On Sep 3, one Azure region took ChatGPT, Claude and Grok down together. We make sure your AI features keep running on infrastructure that fails independently, using fallback models that have already passed your evals."

- **Events:** Azure East US outage (Sep 3) and Anthropic multi-model incident (Jul 29). Neutral gateways absorbed: Stripe→OpenRouter, PANW→Portkey (May 29). Strong open models (Gemma 4) make self-hosted fallback realistic.
- **Buyer:** VP Eng / SRE lead at AI-native SaaS and at companies where an LLM sits in checkout, support or ops (agents doing real work).
- **Buyers × ACV:** ~5,000 companies × $80K (software plus reserved fallback capacity margin) ≈ $0.4B software, more with capacity resale. Reaching $1B would need the capacity business, so this is borderline on the bar.
- **Product:** dependency map (which provider runs in which cloud and region), continuous eval of fallback models per prompt or agent, a pre-warmed fallback pool on separate infrastructure, automatic failover with quality gates, and an SLA.
- **Why now:** the correlated failure is public and recent, and independent routers just got acquired by strategic owners.
- **Why incumbents can't:** labs won't advertise their shared-cloud risk. Hyperscalers fail over only inside their own cloud. Stripe/PANW-owned gateways have other priorities.
- **90-day pilot:** game-day test that kills the primary provider for one production agent. Target: under 1 minute to failover with eval score within 5% of the primary.
- **Kill risks:** LiteLLM/Martian/Not Diamond/Cloudflare/Kong/TrueFoundry already do routing and failover, so the gap may be only the "infrastructure diversity plus eval" feature. Outages may be too rare to justify $80K. Bedrock and Vertex offer multi-model in one place.
- **Verdict:** weakest of the three. Include only if the Top 1/2 interviews fail.

## Summary judgment
Following round 1–5 lessons, most fresh events are **platform absorption** (kill signals). The two openings where startups have not formed come from **new conflicts between enterprises and their platforms**: the agent tollgate (Top 1) and fragmented brand-agent surfaces (Top 2). Both are under 6 months old. In both, the platforms are the counterparty, so they cannot be the neutral solution.
