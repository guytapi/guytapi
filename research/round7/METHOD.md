# Round 7 method (set by the founder)

Start from pain, not ideas. Mine practitioner evidence (Reddit, Hacker News, GitHub issues, engineering blogs, CTO posts, SRE/DevOps/security/AI-engineering communities) from the last 30-60 days (roughly Aug-Oct 2026) for problems created or sharply worsened by adoption of AI agents, coding agents, autonomous engineering, AI-native workflows and AI infrastructure.

Prioritize problems people currently solve with internal scripts, homegrown services, spreadsheets, manual reviews or "we built it internally because nothing existed", and avoid categories with many funded vendors.

Exclusions: another coding agent, generic observability, generic evals, generic orchestration framework, generic agent security, LLM API wrappers.

Per problem: Problem, Recent evidence (5+ independent signals), Who has the pain, What they do today, Why current products fail, Why now, Potential product, Time to value, Pilot (14-30 days), Willingness to pay, Expansion, Competition, Moat (10/100/1,000 customers), CTO test sentence, Kill test question, and scores 1-10 on: Pain severity, Urgency, Market timing, Speed to pilot, Ease of integration, Ease of reaching customers, Willingness to pay, Competition, Moat potential, Market size, VC attractiveness.

If one problem is genuinely strong: stop generating, go deep on it.
