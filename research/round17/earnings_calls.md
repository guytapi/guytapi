## 3. Top 1: Proof-of-capture network for refund, warranty and damage claims (non-insurance)

**Sentence for a VC:** "Generative images broke photo-based refunds and warranty claims, detectors run below chance, and we replace 'upload a photo' with hardened capture plus a cross-merchant reuse graph for retailers, OEMs and carriers."

- **Problem:** Instant-refund, warranty and damage-claim workflows all rely on a customer photo, and AI now produces convincing damage in seconds. Post-hoc detectors score below chance on current generators and give 10%+ false positives (Switch Labs, Aug 2026).
- **Recent evidence (5+ independent signals):** Boll & Branch and Bogg (Modern Retail, Mar 2026); Forter's $800K ring (May 2026); PYMNTS on fake drop-off receipts (2026); Taobao/Tmall built an in-house model (Jan 2026); auto-warranty case of 29 reused-image claims worth $350K (Truepic blog); UPS Happy Returns adding AI "to reduce returns fraud"; Pindrop's sub-threshold $21 bot refunds. **Missing:** no non-tech earnings call names this as a cost, so it sits below CFO-level visibility.
- **Who has the pain:** Heads of CX ops, returns and loss prevention at e-commerce brands with $50M+ online revenue; warranty directors at OEMs (auto, power equipment, appliances, HVAC); claims teams at parcel carriers, 3PLs and freight brokers.
- **What they do today:** Manual review, "keep the item" refund caps, requiring the item back (expensive), Forter/Signifyd behavioral scores, and in-house rules.
- **Why current products fail:** Truepic is sold to insurers and lenders and has a heavy SDK. Insurance-focused detectors (Vaarhaft, PhotoDetective) are pixel classifiers that lose the arms race. Forter scores the person, not the evidence. No one shares image reuse *across* merchants and OEMs.
- **Why now:** Image models crossed photorealism in 2025-26; instant refunds are the norm; C2PA signing ships in phones (Pixel/Samsung) but claimant flows ignore it.
- **Product:** A drop-in capture link or widget (web, no app install) with live-capture checks, sensor and geo/time consistency, C2PA verification where present, plus a shared perceptual-hash graph across customers that flags reused or stock images. Decisions go back into the returns, warranty or claims system (Loop, Happy Returns, Tavant, Syncron, Salesforce).
- **Time to value:** Days. Swap the upload step for a capture link.
- **Pilot (14-30 days):** One brand's damage-claim queue at 10% traffic. Measure the drop in claims (deterrence), confirmed reuse hits, and refund dollars saved.
- **Willingness to pay:** Per verified capture ($0.25-1.00) or a share of savings. Mid-market brand ~$25-60K/yr; OEM warranty program $100-300K/yr.
- **Expansion:** Retail returns → OEM warranty → freight/parcel damage → proof-of-delivery and field-service completion photos → (excluded) insurance via partners.
- **Competition:** Truepic (closest; could pivot downmarket in a quarter), Forter/Signifyd/Riskified (own the merchant relationship and network), Happy Returns (UPS-owned, in-house), Claimlane, Switch Labs, Vaarhaft, Taobao-style in-house builds, Loop/Narvar adding a feature.
- **Moat:** 10 customers give nothing. 100 customers give a cross-merchant reuse graph with some signal. 1,000 customers give a network asset, though Forter already has a comparable cross-merchant network in identity space.
- **CTO test sentence:** "Replace your photo-upload step with our link, and reused or synthetic damage evidence stops getting paid."
- **Kill test question:** Will 3 of 10 brands with >$100M online revenue say AI-faked damage claims exceed $250K/yr, and will Forter/Signifyd not ship capture within 6 months?

## 4. Top 2: Out-of-band instruction verification for money and goods redirection

**Sentence for a VC:** "AI voice and email impersonation now redirects wires, freight and refunds (two 10-Qs this year disclosed losses), and we make every high-risk change instruction require a signed confirmation from the counterparty's registered device."

- **Problem:** Deepfaked executives, vendors and brokers persuade staff to change bank details, reroute loads or issue refunds. Callback controls fail because the callback number is spoofed too.
- **Evidence:** SCI Engineered Materials 10-Q ($898K imposter scam, Feb 2026); JBG SMITH 10-Q ("AI-enabled employee impersonation", Q1 2026); Pindrop retail bot refunds; deepfake broker voices and +31% identity-based cargo theft (Q1 2026); AT&T impersonation scams (round 12). These are 5 signals across 4 sectors.
- **Who has the pain:** Controllers and AP leads, freight brokers' carrier-ops teams, contact-center fraud leads.
- **Today:** Callback policies, Eftsure/Trustpair bank-account validation, Highway carrier identity, Pindrop voice scoring, security awareness training (Adaptive).
- **Why current products fail:** Each tool covers one channel (AP, freight or voice). None gives a *two-sided* signed confirmation that the counterparty has enrolled in.
- **Product:** A counterparty enrolls a device once. Any change instruction above a threshold (bank details, delivery address, refund) becomes a push-signed approval, exposed as an API inside ERP/AP, TMS and contact-center tools.
- **Time to value / pilot:** 30 days for one AP team's vendor bank-change flow. Success = 60%+ vendor enrollment and zero unverified changes.
- **WTP:** $20-80K/yr mid-market, $150K+ enterprise.
- **Expansion:** AP → treasury → freight reroutes → customer refunds → a shared "verified counterparty" directory.
- **Competition:** Eftsure ($), Trustpair, Paymode-X (Bottomline), Coupa/SAP Ariba vendor portals, Highway (freight), Adaptive, Doppel, Pindrop, Okta/Ping verified-ID. Round 12 Z (cross-channel trust) already died here.
- **Moat:** A two-sided enrollment network grows at 100+ customers, but it suffers cold start (a marketplace-like trait the founders want to avoid).
- **CTO test:** "No money or freight moves on a changed instruction unless the counterparty signs it on their enrolled device."
- **Kill test:** Will vendors enroll without a mandate, and does Eftsure/Trustpair already offer device-signed confirmation?

## 5. Lessons for STATUS.md
1. **Non-tech earnings calls are a poor source of AI pain.** Management frames AI as upside (Target, Lowe's, Expedia, Walmart, P&G). Costs surface in 10-Q incident disclosures (SCI, JBG SMITH) and in vendor statistics, which specialists read too.
2. **"Detection loses, capture wins"** is a real technical shift (detectors below chance, Aug 2026). It favors provenance-at-capture products, but Truepic has held that position since 2015 and has not scaled beyond insurance and lending, which is a warning about market size.
3. **Agentic traffic is still "small" in management's own words** (Target, Expedia) in Q2 2026. That confirms pre-demand for agent-commerce ideas until at least 2027.
