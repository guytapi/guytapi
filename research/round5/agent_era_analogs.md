# Round 5: Agent-era platform-shift analog map

Date: 2026-10-05. Method: map every cloud/SaaS-era $10B horizontal category to its AI-agent-era analog, then check crowding (funded startups plus platform features) as of late 2026. 38 web searches. Every URL below came from a search result. Facts marked "(unverified)" are from vendor blogs or aggregators, or from prior knowledge that was not re-checked this round.

**Pass criteria:** (1) a $10B leader is plausible, (2) the current leaders have raised under $30M or don't exist, (3) someone is paying for it today.

## Headline

The analog map confirms what rounds 1-4 found. **Most horizontal agent-era analogs already have a leader with $30M to $200M of funding, or a platform has shipped the feature.** Identity, payments, observability, security, integrations, spend, billing, certification and HR-for-agents are all taken. Only four categories pass both "under $30M" and "real demand today":

1. **SOX / ICFR controls over AI agents that touch the books** (Pathlock/AuditBoard analog). Best fit with the bar.
2. **AI-authored IP assurance**: provenance and clean title for AI-written code and assets (Black Duck/Carta analog). Real pain in M&A, but incumbents have adjacent products.
3. **Liability and dispute evidence for agent-made purchases** (Chargeflow/escrow analog). The pain is starting, but card networks and Forter are moving in.

A fourth, **DocuSign for agent-to-agent agreements**, is open but has no demand yet (186 transactions in Anthropic's Project Deal pilot).

## Top 3

### #1 Agent ICFR: "SOX controls for AI agents that touch the books"
- **One sentence:** "Finance teams are letting AI agents post journal entries, apply cash and run reconciliations; we are the control system that lets auditors sign off on them."
- **Buyer:** Corporate Controller / CAO, with VP Internal Audit or the SOX PMO as co-buyer. The external auditor (Big 4 / national firms) is the forcing function. The buyer is the company itself, not a bank or insurer: it sells to any SOX-404 filer.
- **Buyers x ACV:** roughly 4,000-6,000 US SOX-404 filers plus about 2,000 IPO-track companies and foreign filers (estimate, unverified). At $75K-$200K ACV the core market is about $0.5-1.5B. The path to $5B+ is to extend the same control system to every agent-run regulated business process: SOC 1 for service organizations, revenue and payroll, ISO 42001, and the EU AI Act. Over time it becomes the system of record for how agents are qualified, changed and monitored, which is the ServiceNow-GRC analog.
- **Why now:** agents moved from drafting to executing close tasks in 2026. COSO published GenAI control guidance in Feb 2026 (unverified, aggregator source). PCAOB inspections focus on AI-touched ITGCs and service-account reviews, and "who reviewed the service accounts" is now a standard walkthrough question (unverified, kognitos blog). New PCAOB standards cited for fiscal years beginning on or after 15 Dec 2026 (unverified). Firms like Uniqus are publishing playbooks (Sep 2026). The first audit cycle with agents in the close is FY2026 year-end, which is **Jan-Mar 2027**.
- **Why incumbents can't:** Pathlock and SafePaaS model user roles in ERPs, not non-deterministic agents with prompts, models and tools that change weekly. Optro and Workiva store evidence but don't produce it. Agent vendors (Savant, Rillet, Basis, Numeric, SAP Joule, NetSuite) each log only their own agents, and auditors want one independent control layer across all of them. The Big 4 can't sell it because of auditor-independence rules.
- **90-day pilot:** at a recent IPO or mid-cap filer that runs 3-10 finance agents: (1) build an inventory and risk-rate every agent touching in-scope accounts; (2) set up change control on model, prompt and tool versions; (3) capture tamper-evident evidence for each agent action; (4) run a mock walkthrough with the external auditor. Success means the auditor accepts the evidence package for one key control in the Q4 or year-end audit.
- **Kill risk:** (a) auditors accept vendor-native logs, so no separate layer is needed; (b) Pathlock, Optro or Workiva ships "agent controls" as a module in 2027; (c) adoption of finance agents in public companies is slower than the hype, so only a few hundred filers have in-scope agents in 2027. **Test first:** interview 10 controllers and 5 audit partners about whether agents are in scope for FY2026 and what evidence auditors are asking for.

### #2 AI-authored IP assurance: "clean title for AI-written software"
- **One sentence:** "Half of new code is written by AI and acquirers are cutting multiples over it; we certify what was AI-generated, its license exposure and its copyrightability, continuously and at deal time."
- **Buyer:** GC or CTO at software companies with $20M+ ARR that are preparing to raise or sell; corp dev and PE tech-diligence teams; enterprise procurement asking vendors for attestations.
- **Buyers x ACV:** about 5,000-10,000 software companies with $20M+ ARR, plus PE firms and corp-dev teams (estimate, unverified). At $50K-$150K ACV that is about $0.5-1B. A $10B outcome needs this to become a standard required in every software contract (the SBOM-like "AI-BOM for authorship"), which is uncertain.
- **Why now:** acquirers are already walking away from deals and cutting multiples (Bain via saas.group), license conflicts are rising sharply (OSSRA 2026), and the first heavily vibe-coded companies are reaching M&A.
- **Why incumbents can't:** Black Duck and Sonar match snippets and check quality. They don't reconstruct authorship (which agent, which prompt, how much human contribution) or produce a copyrightability opinion.
- **90-day pilot:** a full-history provenance and IP-risk report for one PE portfolio company ahead of a sale, plus continuous monitoring in its CI.
- **Kill risk:** coding tools already write authorship into commits (Co-Authored-By trailers), so provenance becomes free metadata. Black Duck bundles it into its existing M&A audits. Demand is episodic, deal-driven and below $50K.

### #3 Agent-commerce liability and dispute evidence (weak)
- **One sentence:** "When an AI agent buys something and the human says 'I didn't do that', we prove what was authorized and who pays."
- **Buyer:** VP Payments / Risk at large merchants and marketplaces.
- **Buyers x ACV:** about 5,000-10,000 merchants with $100M+ GMV (estimate) at $50K-$250K ACV, which is about $1B.
- **Why now:** Visa, Mastercard and Amex activated agent payments in 2026. No rule assigns fault yet, so merchants pay by default.
- **Why incumbents can't:** chargeback tools see only card data. The evidence lives in the agent session and its mandate. This argument is weak: Forter launched Forter Agents in June 2026.
- **90-day pilot:** capture agent mandates and session evidence on agent-originated orders at one merchant, then measure the dispute win rate.
- **Kill risk:** high. Card networks write the liability rules into the protocols (Visa TAP, Google AP2, Amex coverage), and Forter, Riskified and Chargeflow add the evidence. Volume is still small (Juniper estimates about $8B of agentic commerce in 2026).

## Bottom line
Of the horizontal agent-era analogs, only **Agent ICFR (#1)** meets all of: under $30M of funding in the category, money being spent today (audit-driven), $50K+ ACV, thousands of buyers, a 90-day pilot tied to the year-end audit, and no banking, government or insurance buyers. Its weak spots are the $10B ceiling (it has to expand from SOX to every agent-run regulated process) and the risk that GRC/IGA incumbents absorb it. Validate it with interviews of controllers and audit partners before a deep dive.
