## 3. Top 2 near-misses: full finalist format
These are the two items closest to the bar. They are written up because the brief asked for it, not because either one qualifies.

### Finalist 1: AI operating layer for electrical/MEP subcontractors on data-center and battery builds ("the durable tech asset Conviction couldn't find")
- **One-line problem:** Electrical and MEP subcontractors are the bottleneck on AI data-center and BESS builds. They run estimating, submittals, QA/QC and commissioning paperwork with spreadsheets and scarce foremen, so schedules slip and margin leaks.
- **Why now:** The data-center capex wave (Meta $115-135B for 2026; Aligned $13B for 2026, from search snippets) runs into a shortage of electricians. Guo said on Sep 1 2026 that she looked at builders and installers and found operations businesses, not product assets.
- **Exact buyer:** COO or VP Operations at a US electrical/MEP subcontractor with $100M-$2B revenue doing mission-critical work.
- **Exact ICP:** Roughly 300-600 US mission-critical-capable electrical contractors [estimate, unverified]. Each has 3+ active data-center or BESS projects and 200+ field electricians.
- **Current workaround:** Procore/ACC for documents, Excel for takeoffs, in-house QA/QC binders, commissioning agents' forms, and overtime.
- **Why incumbents can't easily own it:** Procore and Autodesk serve GCs and owners, not the sub's own P&L. Hyperscalers care about the schedule, not the sub's margin. Weak argument: Procore can ship trade-specific agents.
- **30-day MVP:** An agent that turns drawings and specs into a takeoff, submittal package and QA/QC checklist for one scope (switchgear and busway), checked against the sub's past jobs.
- **Pilot design:** Two subs, one live data-center project each. Measure estimator hours per bid, submittal rejection rate and punch items at commissioning against their last comparable project.
- **Pricing hypothesis:** $60-150K/yr per contractor platform fee plus a per-project fee (~0.1% of electrical contract value).
- **Expansion path:** All MEP trades → GCs → owners (a commissioning data layer) → workforce/dispatch → a roll-up of subs (the "AI-native contractor" model).
- **Moat:** Job-cost and QA/QC history across projects, which predicts productivity per scope. Weak until there are dozens of contractors.
- **Why $10B+ (stretch):** Only through the roll-up path, i.e. owning contractors. That is exactly the "operations + real estate financing" shape Guo declined.
- **Direct competitors and adjacent threats:** Rebar ($14M A, trades vertical AI), Probook (Sequoia), XBuild, Procore AI agents, Autodesk Construction Cloud, ServiceTitan (commercial), Kojo/Trimble (materials) [Kojo/Trimble from memory, unverified].
- **Sentence to send the CFO:** "We cut estimator hours per data-center bid by half and submittal rejections by a third. Your foremen stop doing paperwork and you take on one more project a year with the same crews."
- **5 discovery questions:**
  1. How many bids did you decline last year for lack of estimators or foremen?
  2. What did your last data-center project lose to rework or rejected submittals, in dollars?
  3. Which tools do your PMs actually open daily, and which did you stop paying for?
  4. Who signs a $100K software contract, and against which budget line?
  5. Would the GC or owner give you access to their commissioning data if it reduced your punch list?
- **Hard kill criteria:**
  - Fewer than 2 of 8 contractors put a dollar figure above $500K/yr on bid capacity or rework.
  - Procore or Autodesk already ships the same takeoff/submittal agent to their sub accounts.
  - No buyer will name a budget line.
- **Scores:** Pain 7, Urgency 7, ROI clarity 6, Access 5, Pilot speed 4 (project cycles), Market size 6, Expansion 6, Venture potential 5, Defensibility 4, Why now 7, Competition 4. **Avg 5.5.**
- **Classification: KILL.** More than 2 funded players. The partner signal is "not investable", not "underbuilt". It overlaps R2/S and is outside the founders' edge.
- **14-day test (only to reverse the kill):** Get 10 calls with mission-critical electrical subs. Pass = 4+ quantify ≥$500K/yr of lost bids or rework, AND 2 share a past bid package for a back-test, AND none uses Rebar/XBuild/Procore AI today.

### Finalist 2: Company-to-company agent handshake (Batko's "AI brains that talk to other AI brains")
- **One-line problem:** When a buyer's agent and a supplier's or partner's agent need to coordinate, nothing standard covers identity, scope, what each side may disclose, or a shared record of what was agreed.
- **Why now:** Agents run most of the sourcing events at some buyers (Keelvar ~71%, round 20). A2A/LCP protocols exist, but no neutral clearing layer does.
- **Exact buyer:** Head of procurement operations or the CIO's agent platform team at a $5B+ manufacturer or distributor.
- **Exact ICP:** Companies with 1,000+ suppliers where agents already run negotiations or RFQs.
- **Current workaround:** Email, supplier portals, and buyer-platform-native agent logs.
- **Why incumbents can't easily own it:** A neutral cross-platform role. But round 20 found DocuSign, Coupa, Ariba and Integra Ledger each a step away.
- **30-day MVP:** A policy-scoped handshake API plus a dual-signed transcript for agent-to-agent RFQ exchanges between one buyer and 5 suppliers.
- **Pilot design:** One buyer and 10 suppliers on tail-spend RFQs. Measure cycle time and the dispute rate.
- **Pricing hypothesis:** $50K/yr per buyer plus per-handshake fees paid by suppliers.
- **Expansion path:** Procurement → logistics → partner channels → a cross-industry agent identity network.
- **Moat:** Two-sided network of verified agent identities and transaction history.
- **Why $10B+:** Only if it becomes the agent-era EDI/VAN. That is pre-demand.
- **Direct competitors and adjacent threats:** AAA Legal Context Protocol, Google A2A, DocuSign, Keelvar, Pactum, Coupa, Ariba, Experian Agent Registry, Visa/Mastercard KYA.
- **Sentence to send the CTO:** "When your agent negotiates with a supplier's agent, we are the shared, signed record of who was authorized to agree to what."
- **5 discovery questions:**
  1. How many supplier interactions are agent-to-agent today, by count per month?
  2. Has an agent-made commitment been disputed, and what did it cost?
  3. Who owns the agent-to-agent policy internally?
  4. Would your platform vendor's native log satisfy audit?
  5. Would suppliers pay to be "verified-agent" listed?
- **Hard kill criteria:**
  - Fewer than 50 agent-to-agent exchanges/month at a design partner.
  - The buyer says the Coupa/Ariba native log is enough.
  - No supplier is willing to pay.
- **Scores:** Pain 4, Urgency 4, ROI clarity 4, Access 5, Pilot speed 5, Market size 7, Expansion 7, Venture potential 7, Defensibility 6, Why now 6, Competition 4. **Avg 5.4.**
- **Classification: KILL.** It duplicates the round-20 B finalist (counter-signature, 6.5), which remains stronger, and the round-22 B+ and round-23 kills. The source is not a top-firm partner.
- **14-day test:** None new. Use the round-20 counter-signature test instead.

## 4. Conclusions
1. **Partners at top firms rarely say "underbuilt" in public in 2026.** They talk up their portfolio themes. When they say they looked and passed (Guo on DC builders and installers), the reason is business-model quality, not absence of startups.
2. **The partner-quote method adds a 17th confirmation of the STATUS.md structural conclusion:** what is publicly named is already funded.
3. **If this method is repeated, it needs transcript access** (podscripts, Colossus or Snipd transcripts via a working fetch) or direct partner conversations. Snippet-level search cannot supply 25 verified in-window quotes.
4. **Watch item:** the Benchmark "What We Believe Now" Disrupt session ("opportunities still hiding in plain sight"). Pull its transcript after it airs.
5. **Next steps:** no new A or B. The best open item is still the round-20 B finalist (counter-signature for machine-to-machine deals).
