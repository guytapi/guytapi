**Method:** generate 10 hypotheses outside H1-H10 and the killed list; push each one to 2028-29; go one layer down to look for an infrastructure category; check competitors quickly; kill hard using the standing lessons. **25 web searches** (cap 35). WebFetch and Reddit were blocked, so every fact comes from a search snippet. **[U]** marks a claim that rests on one snippet or on my own inference. Every URL below appeared in a search result. **Lessons used as kill tests:** (L1) platform absorption; (L2) visible pain draws a race of entrants; (L3) the money goes to the licensed signer, not the record keeper; (L4) anything a platform team can build in a sprint is dead; (L5) destination subsidy: whoever receives the spend gives the rail away free; (L6) vendors treat memory, config and review as their moat and keep it in-house; plus pre-demand (no behavior yet) and neutral-record failure.

## Result in one line

**No A. One weak, conditional B (H15, "delegated inference clearing", 6.0) at roughly 10% odds. Nine kills.** The pattern from rounds 28-29 holds one interface further out. Where contracts meet runtime, Transcend, Vanta and the AI gateways already sell the controls. Where vendors meet model providers, the providers are making themselves the billing and abuse-control rail (OpenAI "Sign in with ChatGPT", 29 Sep 2026).

## Part 2. Push each to 2028-29, go one layer deeper, check competitors, verdict

### H11 Subprocessor lock → KILL
- **2028-29 version:** vendors route each request across 8-15 model endpoints (frontier, open-weight, regional, on-device) and change the mix monthly. Each enterprise DPA has its own notice period (30 days to 6 months), objection right, or "no new subprocessor without consent" clause. Even the model owners churn subprocessor lists: Microsoft cut its AI-subprocessor notice from six months to 30 days in May 2026 and then added Anthropic and OpenAI as Copilot subprocessors. That squeezes every processor that had promised its own customers six months. ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/microsofts-dpa-update-cuts-ai-subprocessor-notice-to-30-days/))
- **Layer below:** an "allowed-model set per tenant", computed from each contract and enforced at the router, with notices generated automatically and objections tracked.
- **Competitors:** notice drafting already exists (icompaas **LLM Switchboard** has a "subprocessor notice" use case: [llm-switchboard.icompaas.com](https://llm-switchboard.icompaas.com/app/usecases/subprocessor_notice)). DPA clause tracking exists (**ConductAtlas** indexes OpenAI/Mistral/Perplexity subprocessor and objection terms: [conductatlas.com](https://conductatlas.com/platform/mistral-ai/mistral-ai-data-processing-addendum/subprocessor-objection-and-termination-right/)). Customer commitments are tracked in **Vanta "Managing Customer Commitments"** ([help.vanta.com](https://help.vanta.com/en/articles/13931299-managing-customer-commitments)). Per-tenant allowlists sit in gateways (see H12).
- **Kill:** L4 (once commitments are structured, the router join is a sprint), plus L1 (Vanta/trust centers). The pain is real but settles into compliance tooling.

### H12 Contract terms never reach the runtime → KILL
- **2028-29 version:** every SaaS company has hundreds of AI features and every enterprise contract has an AI rider, which makes obligations a combinatorial policy problem enforced at the router, memory, eval and logging layers.
- **Layer below:** a "contract-to-runtime compiler": clauses become machine policy that propagates to the gateway, vector store, trace store and eval pipeline, with proof of enforcement.
- **Competitors:**
  - **Transcend** extended "Do Not Train" and "Deep Deletion" specifically "for B2B AI companies", with record-level no-train guarantees that fulfil "contractual obligations" ([martechseries](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/transcend-expands-do-not-train-and-deep-deletion-to-power-responsible-ai-at-scale-for-b2b-ai-companies/); [transcend.io blog](https://transcend.io/blog/do-not-train-deep-deletion-power-responsible-ai-at-scale)).
  - **Vercel AI Gateway** offers team-wide and per-request ZDR ([vercel.com](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/security-and-compliance/zdr)), and **OpenRouter** pitches residency "at the routing layer" ([openrouter.ai](https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/ai-data-residency/)).
  - **Gravitee** has per-tenant policy decision points and residency routing ([gravitee.io](https://gravitee.io/corpus/gen-971/winter-was-hard/policy-driven-data-residency-and-regional-routing-for-ai-agent-and-llm-traffic.html)). **Trussed AI** sells runtime policy enforcement ([trussed.ai](https://trussed.ai/resources/ai-agent-runtime-governance-for-enterprise-security)). Sirion (CLM) markets contract-governed AI training data ([sirion.ai](https://www.sirion.ai/library/contract-insights/contract-systems-ai-data-governance/)).
  - Academic prior art: "Contract-Centered Architecture for Agentic Runtimes" (arXiv 2608.27086).
- **Kill:** L1 + L2. Every layer of the stack has an owner, and the remaining clause-to-policy extraction is an LLM feature for CLM vendors.

### H13 Offboarding deletion of AI-derived artifacts → KILL
- **2028-29 version:** fine-tuned weights trained on several customers' data, persistent agent memory, and eval sets built from production traces make an honest deletion certificate close to impossible. Machine unlearning would become a contractual requirement.
- **Layer below:** lineage from tenant data into derived artifacts, plus an unlearning or retraining service.
- **Competitors:** **Transcend Deep Deletion** with "verifiable proof of deletion" (above); lineage writers (RudderStack, a USPTO lineage-aware retention patent). Vendors are also avoiding cross-customer fine-tunes altogether (OpenAI DPA deletion terms: [conductatlas.com](https://conductatlas.com/platform/openai/openai-data-processing-addendum/data-return-or-deletion-on-termination/)). The fine-tune problem is publicly discussed ([tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/05/10/right-to-erasure-fine-tuning-deletion-stops-at-snapshot)).
- **Kill:** L1 (Transcend), and vendors are designing around it with per-tenant adapters instead of shared weights, so the unlearning market stays thin **[U]**.

### H14 Upstream kill switch → KILL (near miss)
- **Evidence the behavior exists:** OpenAI community threads show org-level terminations caused by key holders or end users, including an admission that OpenAI "sent violation warnings to too many organizations by mistake" ([community.openai.com](https://community.openai.com/t/false-child-exploitation-policy-violation-warning-from-openai/1143362); [community.openai.com](https://community.openai.com/t/access-terminated-due-to-exploitation-harm-or-sexualization-of-children/1155074)). Foundation-model AUPs contain 127 distinct restrictions and include remote-enforcement rights (AAAI AIES paper: [ojs.aaai.org](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/view/31677)).
- **2028-29 version:** AI apps serve millions of end users through two or three provider orgs, provider abuse classifiers grow stricter (bio/cyber), and one false positive becomes an extinction event. The layer below would be an "upstream compliance firewall": classify downstream traffic against each provider's AUP, isolate keys per customer, keep an appeal evidence pack, and fail over to a more permissive provider.
- **Kill:** L1. The fix belongs to the provider and the gateway: per-project keys, end-user safety identifiers, free moderation endpoints, and gateway multi-provider failover (Portkey/OpenRouter/LiteLLM). Content-safety classifiers are the crowded "AI security/T&S" space (Lakera, ActiveFence, Cloudflare). The round-13 "fraud layer for AI companies" kill also applies. **Tripwire:** a public multi-day outage of a well-known AI app caused by provider account suspension.

### H16 Back-to-back SLA gap → KILL
- **Layer below:** an inference capacity guarantee or SLA wrap.
- **Kill:** multi-provider failover is gateway-commodity (L1/L4), reserved capacity is sold by the providers, and the SLA-insurance flank belongs to Parametrix/AIG (the round-28 Verified Uptime kill, L3). The snippets about the gap are generic SEO pages ([tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/19/foundation-model-vendor-strategy-sla-pricing-enterprise)). ### H17 Bake-off tax → KILL
- **Layer below:** a buyer-side proving ground where vendors run head-to-head on the buyer's data.
- **Kill:** purchases are episodic (low frequency, so no compounding); benchmark orchestration exists (**Runloop** benchmark platform with W&B: [runloop.ai](https://runloop.ai/media/runloop-launches-ai-benchmark-orchestration-platform-with-weights-biases-integration)); Vals and LMArena own neutral benchmarks (round 10); and vendors refuse neutral grading of their configs (L6). ### H18 Customer-agent load → KILL (pre-demand)
- No evidence of agent-originated ticket volume or gaming of per-resolution pricing turned up. ### H19 Agent-induced spend in customer systems → KILL
- The behavior is real: "Wait, my Snowflake bill did what?" covers agentic Cortex costs, and Uber burned through its 2026 AI budget in four months ([revefi.com](https://www.revefi.com/blog/wait-my-snowflake-bill-did-what-the-hidden-cost-of-agentic-ai-in-snowflake)).

### H20 Retroactive audit after model deprecation → KILL

- **Kill:** this is a neutral-record thesis (L3; round-28 merge kill). Black-box logs are free OSS. ## Part 3. Finalist write-up: H15 "Delegated inference clearing"

**One-line problem.** As enterprises push SaaS vendors to run AI features on the enterprise's own model contracts, there is no neutral way to grant a vendor scoped, metered, policy-bound use of a customer's inference capacity across providers. As a result each vendor hand-builds BYOK per customer and absorbs a support and quality burden it cannot price.

**Why this problem exists now.**
- 2026 is the first year enterprise SaaS shipped BYOK/BYOM broadly: GitHub Copilot (Jun 2026), DeepSource, Alation (Aug 2026), infrrd.
- Enterprises have large token commits (ChatGPT Enterprise token billing; Anthropic ~40% of enterprise model spend per secondary sources **[U]**) and want every AI dollar to count against them, the same reason cloud marketplaces won SaaS procurement.
- OpenAI made the consumer version a rail on 29 Sep 2026 (Sign in with ChatGPT). That proves the economic pull, and it is also the main threat.

**Exact buyer.** Head of AI Platform / CTO at a SaaS vendor (Series C to public, 200+ enterprise accounts, AI features embedded across the product), with the CFO as co-sponsor because inference COGS leaves the P&L. A secondary buyer on the enterprise side is the CIO/FinOps owner of an inference commit above $5M.

**Exact ICP.** B2B SaaS vendors in document AI, code/devtools, data/analytics, ITSM and CX whose enterprise RFPs now include a BYOK/BYOM clause, who serve 2+ customer providers (Azure OpenAI, Bedrock, Anthropic direct, Vertex), and who have 10+ BYOK tenants live.

**Current workaround.** Each vendor builds per-tenant key vaulting, routing and tenant metering in-house, or uses a gateway's BYOK mode (OpenRouter, ORQ, Portkey). Each vendor also keeps a private matrix of "supported customer models" and handles breakages ad hoc when a customer's deployment pins an old model version or runs out of quota.

**Why incumbents cannot easily own it.**
- A model provider can only clear its own models, while enterprises hold 2-3 provider contracts.
- Clouds clear only marketplace-listed spend inside their own cloud.
- Gateways sit on one side (the vendor's) and do not carry the enterprise's grant, quota, policy and audit to the other side.
- The only neutral position is a two-sided grant: enterprise ↔ vendor ↔ provider. **Honest caveat:** this argument is the weakest part. L5 says the provider whose commit is drawn down has every reason to give this rail away free, and OpenAI already did for consumers.

**30-day MVP.**
- An SDK plus control plane for vendors. The enterprise admin issues a scoped "inference grant" (models allowed, monthly token cap, region, ZDR flag, expiry) on its Azure/Bedrock/Anthropic/OpenAI account.
- The vendor's app calls through the grant.
- A conformance suite runs the vendor's own eval set against the customer's exact deployed model versions and flags drift before go-live and on every provider version change.
- Both sides see the same per-feature metering statement.

**Pilot design.**
- Two SaaS vendors, each with at least 5 BYOK tenants, over 6 weeks.
- Measure (a) BYOK-related support tickets and engineering hours per tenant before and after, (b) incidents caused by quota or model-version mismatches caught before customers saw them, (c) time to onboard a new BYOK tenant.
- Pass: 50% fewer BYOK tickets, onboarding under 1 day, and 1 enterprise admin willing to issue grants to a second vendor through the same control plane (the network test).

**Pricing hypothesis.** $2-5K per month per vendor platform fee plus $200-500 per BYOK tenant per month. Later, 0.5-1% of cleared inference spend once enterprise-side grants carry it. That beats OpenRouter's 5% BYOK fee only if the enterprise side brings network value.

**Expansion path.**
1. Vendor-side BYOK ops.
2. The enterprise-side "inference wallet": one place to grant, cap and audit every SaaS vendor's use of company inference.
3. Cross-provider clearing and chargeback.
4. Commit-optimization brokerage, if regulation allows (the round-18 ProsperOps kill warns of non-transferability).

**Moat.** A two-sided network (enterprises with grants × vendors that accept grants), a cross-vendor conformance dataset (which vendor features break on which model versions), and position in procurement. All three are weak until both sides reach dozens of participants.

**Why it could become a $10B+ company (and why absence of competitors is plausible).**
- **Absence of competitors is plausible** because the enterprise-side need appeared only in 2026 and is still negotiated deal by deal. No product sells a cross-provider, two-sided grant: gateways are one-sided, and Sign in with ChatGPT covers one provider and consumer plans [U]. The pain arrives *after* signature, as support load, so it is invisible in public sources.
- **The $10B path:** if a large share of SaaS-embedded inference moves to customer-funded contracts by 2029, the clearing layer sits on a flow of tens of billions of dollars a year [U, inference]. That is the AWS Marketplace / Tackle.io pattern (enterprises routing SaaS through commits) combined with the OAuth pattern (delegated authority) for compute. Whoever holds the enterprise-side wallet would also be the control point for agent spend, policy and audit across every AI vendor.
- **Why this is probably wrong:** comparables in commit-drawdown tooling (Tackle, Clazar, Suger) are ~$1B-scale outcomes, not $10B [U]. And the providers and clouds have a strong reason to subsidize the rail (L5).

**Direct competitors and adjacent threats.**
- **OpenAI** Sign in with ChatGPT plus ChatGPT Enterprise token billing (the biggest threat: an enterprise version is one announcement away).
- **AWS/Azure** marketplaces and Bedrock/Foundry cross-account patterns.
- **OpenRouter** BYOK, Portkey, ORQ, LiteLLM, Vercel AI Gateway; Yotta Labs; byok-relay OSS.
- **Anthropic** if it ships a third-party app quota (it did a partial version for OpenClaw-type tools in 2026 [U]).
- Vendor in-house builds (L6: vendors see their model or eval matrix as their moat and may refuse an external conformance suite).

**One sentence to send a CTO.** "Your top enterprise accounts now make you run on *their* model contracts; we turn each one into a scoped, metered inference grant with a conformance check against their exact model versions, so BYOK stops being a per-customer engineering project."

**Hard kill criteria.**
1. OpenAI, Anthropic, AWS or Microsoft announces enterprise-grade third-party app billing or quota grants before the pilot ends, and it is multi-tenant across vendors.
2. Fewer than 15% of ICP vendors' top-50 accounts demand BYOK/commit drawdown in 2026-27 contracts.
3. BYOK support tickets are under 2 per tenant per quarter at pilot vendors (the pain is a toggle after all).
4. No enterprise admin will issue grants to a second vendor through a third-party control plane (no network).
5. A gateway (OpenRouter/Portkey) ships enterprise-side grants within 90 days.

**14-day test (data and pilot only, no interviews).** (a) Scrape the security/AI docs and changelogs of the 100 largest B2B SaaS vendors and count BYOK/BYOM support and announcement dates. Pass if at least 25% have it, with at least 10 launched in the last 6 months. (b) Instrument one design-partner vendor's BYOK tenants for 14 days: tickets, model-version mismatches, quota incidents. Pass if at least 3 mismatch or quota incidents per 10 tenants. (c) Monitor OpenAI, Anthropic, AWS and Microsoft announcements. If any ships enterprise third-party grants, the test ends in a kill.

**Classification: B (weak, conditional), roughly 10% odds. Fails A on Urgency, Defensibility and Competition position.** It ranks below the round-20 counter-signature B (~6.4) on evidence, but it has a clearer money flow: inference COGS is the largest line in AI app P&Ls, and this thesis is about who pays it. It becomes a KILL the moment a model provider or cloud ships enterprise third-party grants (kill criterion 1), which L5 says is the likely outcome.

## Part 4. Implications

1. **The vendor-customer contract interface (H11-H13) is already served.** Transcend went after B2B AI vendors in 2025-26. Vanta tracks customer commitments. Gateways enforce ZDR and residency per team or request. Contract obligations look like a hidden pain, but they get bought as compliance features.
2. **The vendor-provider interface (H14-H16) is being absorbed by the providers.** They own keys, abuse controls, capacity and now the billing rail (Sign in with ChatGPT). The one unresolved slice is cross-provider, two-sided delegation (H15), and it survives only until a provider or cloud ships its enterprise version.
3. **Customer agents vs vendor agents (H18) is still pre-demand**, consistent with rounds 6, 20 and 25.
4. **Tripwires worth watching** (cheap, no interviews):
   - an enterprise version of Sign in with ChatGPT, or an Anthropic/AWS equivalent (kills H15);
   - a public AI-app outage caused by a provider account suspension (reopens H14);
   - a published case of a DPA breach over an undisclosed model-provider switch (reopens H11/H12 as a liability play, though the money would still go to the signer).
