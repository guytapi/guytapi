**What this means for the lens.** The "AI layoff wave" as a source of budget is peaking or being hidden. The durable behavior is (1) hiring freezes and attrition, and (2) quiet rehiring. Freezes create no event to sell into. Rehiring is a lagging cleanup that recruiting and alumni tools already handle. Companies that hide AI as the cause also won't buy a product branded around "AI layoffs".

## 3. Best of the lens, in full finalist format: (b) Coverage verification before cutting

**One-line problem.** Companies cut teams on the promise of an agent's deflection rate, then find the agent never handled the 10-30% of rare, high-stakes cases that only those people knew. Over half regret the cuts and quietly rehire.

**Why now.**
- Spring 2026 had record AI-cited cuts (112K through July).
- In 2026 surveys from Orgvue (55% regret), Gartner (50% rehire by 2027) and Careerminds (32.7% have already rehired 25-50%), companies say they cut too early.
- 20-35% of tasks processed by AI in operations, finance and support still need a human *(stealthagents snippet, unverified)*.
- The AWS outage commentary blamed the loss of veterans' tacit knowledge *(opinion, not proof)*.

**Exact buyer.** COO or the head of the transformation office, with the CFO co-signing. Never the CHRO: the CHRO has the concern but not the budget.

**Exact ICP.** Companies with 2,000-50,000 employees running agent rollouts in service, back office (AP, claims, KYC ops) or IT support, planning to cut 10%+ of a function within 2 quarters. Ideally regulated (insurance, banking ops, healthcare revenue-cycle management), where a missed edge case becomes a reportable incident.

**Current workaround.** In order of how often they are used:
- the agent vendor's own containment or "automation potential" estimate (Zendesk, Freshservice, Sierra/Decagon pilots)
- a consultancy business case (McKinsey, Accenture, Big 4)
- running the agent in parallel for a few weeks
- exit interviews or Sensay-style knowledge capture
- Mimica/Celonis task maps

**Why incumbents might not own it.** The agent vendor is conflicted: its revenue depends on the deflection number. The consultancy is conflicted: it sold the transformation business case. HR tech (Gloat, Orgvue) doesn't see transcripts or tickets.

**Why that reason is weak.** Buyers routinely accept the vendor's own numbers (taxonomy assumption 2). Interloom and Mimica already ingest the same operational records and are a positioning change away. Auditors and Big 4 can sell "independent assurance" as a service.

**30-day MVP.**
1. Ingest 6-12 months of tickets, cases or emails for one function, plus the per-person resolution history.
2. Cluster the cases and find the tail: rare, high-cost, or handled by only 1-2 people.
3. Replay a stratified sample against the customer's chosen agent.
4. Output a "safe-to-cut" coverage report: residual human hours by case cluster, a list of single-point-of-knowledge people, and a targeted knowledge-capture plan for those people before their exit date.

**Pilot design.** One function at one design partner, in 3 weeks. Back-test on a team that has *already* been cut: would the report have flagged the cases that caused the rehiring? Pass if it identifies ≥70% of the post-cut escalations and incidents in hindsight.

**Pricing hypothesis.** $40-80K per function assessment, plus $50-150K a year for continuous residual monitoring after the cut. Possibly priced as a percentage of the avoided cost of rehiring.

**Expansion path.** Function → enterprise transformation office → continuous "human residual" monitoring of every agent → insurer or auditor attestation that a restructuring is "AI-coverage verified".

**Moat.** A cross-customer benchmark of which case types agents actually fail at, by industry and vendor. This is weak: the data is per customer and sensitive, and the agent vendors hold larger datasets.

**Why it could become a $10B+ company.** Only if "coverage attestation" became a required step before AI restructurings, for boards, insurers, or regulators under a future AI-Related Job Impacts Clarity Act. None of those exist today.

**Direct competitors and adjacent threats.**
- Interloom (context graph of tacit decisions)
- Mimica (task mining for agents)
- Zendesk and Freshservice automation-potential reports
- Orgvue (owns the "AI regret" research narrative)
- TechWolf
- Sensay
- Celonis
- the agents' own eval and simulation suites
- McKinsey, Accenture, Big 4 assurance

**One sentence to send a CFO.** "Before you take the $X million out of [function], we'll replay last year's cases against your agent and show you exactly which cases, and which people, the savings case is missing, so you don't join the 55% who rehire."

**5 discovery questions.**
1. When you last cut a team after an agent rollout, what broke in the next 90 days, and what did it cost?
2. Whose deflection or containment number justified the cut, and did anyone check it independently?
3. Have you rehired, re-contracted, or moved work to a BPO for any role you cut citing AI? How much?
4. Who signs off that a function is "safe to cut" today, and from whose budget would you pay for verification?
5. Would you give us 6 months of ticket and case data for a back-test on a team you've already cut?

**Hard kill criteria.**
- <2 of 10 COO/CFO conversations admit a costly post-cut failure.
- Nobody will share data for a back-test.
- Buyers say "the vendor's pilot is enough".
- Willingness to pay is below $40K for a one-time assessment, with no appetite for recurring monitoring.

**Scores.**

| Pain | Urgency | ROI clarity | Accessibility | Pilot speed | Market size | Expansion | Venture potential | Defensibility | Why now | Competition position | **Avg** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 5 | 6 | 4 | 6 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 7 | 5 | **5.3** |

**Why each score is low.**
- **Urgency 5.** Cuts that companies attribute to AI collapsed in August, and most firms freeze hiring rather than cut. A freeze needs no verification.
- **Accessibility 4.** The project is politically toxic: nobody wants a vendor associated with layoffs. The buyer who ordered the cut wants confirmation, not a brake.
- **Market size 5.** It is a one-time project per restructuring (violates taxonomy constraint 6). The recurring monitoring piece collides with agent QA/observability vendors (Decagon Watchtower, Fin Monitors; see round 18).
- **Defensibility 4.** Interloom and Mimica already hold the same ingestion pipeline and funding.

**Classification: KILL.** Not B: the behavior is public, widely reported, and partially served. This idea is a version of the "knowledge context for agents" category (Interloom, Mimica) wearing a layoff-risk label.

**Optional 14-day test, only if the founders have warm access to 5+ COOs or CFOs who cut staff after an agent rollout:**
1. Get 2 companies to share ticket data from a function they have already cut.
2. Run the back-test.
3. Pass = it flags ≥70% of post-cut escalations, plus one signed $40K+ assessment for an upcoming cut.

If either part fails, the idea is dead for good.

## 4. Lessons from this lens
1. **Labor-transition pain is real, but it is bought as services and absorbed by HR suites.** Outplacement, comp consultancies, law firms and talent-intelligence platforms each own one slice, and all of them shipped "agentic" versions in H1 2026.
2. **The attributable event is disappearing.** Companies stopped citing AI (Challenger August; NY WARN shows zero AI disclosures). A product that needs the buyer to admit "AI layoff" has no front door.
3. **The durable second-order effect is the hiring freeze, not the layoff.** That moves money away from HR tooling. It is not a new budget line.
4. **The rehiring wave is the most interesting data point** (55% regret, 50% rehire). But its logical products (alumni rehiring, tacit-knowledge capture, task mining) were funded in 2025-26: BoomerangHR, Interloom, Mimica, Sensay. This is taxonomy F2 again.
