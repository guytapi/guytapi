# Round 29 — H4: "The spec writer became the bottleneck"

Date: 2026-10-06. Searches used: 22/22. Sources are listed at the end; any claim not backed by a search result in this session is marked (unverified).

**Verdict: KILL.** The best thesis, the Judgment Ledger and Router, scores **5.6 on average after the red team**. No outcome-B candidate either. The pain is real and well documented. But every layer that money flows to already has an owner: one of the platforms that holds the spec, the flag or the review (GitHub, Linear, Atlassian, LaunchDarkly, Harness, Augment), or a funded specialist (Momentic, Taste Labs, Tessl, Kiro, Brief, cubic, Pensero).

---

## 0. Evidence that the behavior is starting now

- Engineers say the bottleneck has moved. "The bottleneck is no longer the act of writing code. It is the precision of the intent that drives them" (noqta; Stack Overflow/O'Reilly "right amount of spec", Aug 21 2026). The "plan and review" workflow (kunalganglani) describes a senior's day: 90 minutes reviewing overnight agent PRs, then writing the next batch of specs before signing off (semnexus).
- In a survey of 700 practitioners, about 31% of developer time went to invisible work such as reviewing AI code. 81% say review time rose, and 28% say it rose by more than 30% (search snippet; original survey not opened).
- Review quality is breaking down. AI PRs show about 1.7x more issues, and a 22K-developer dataset shows incidents per PR up 242.7%. Review time per AI PR is falling, which reads as rubber-stamping (tianpan.co, Apr 2026).
- Judgment is being removed from the loop instead of scaled. Human sign-off on high-risk agent actions fell from 40% to 25% in six months, and actions with no human review rose from 11% to 26% (Agile Brand Guide, Jul 25 2026).
- New job titles are appearing. Product Engineer postings say "translate into precise specifications, guide AI agents, remain accountable for reviewing agent output". Agent PM roles pay $170-270K (Paraform; DefenseStorm posting via Georgian).
- Benchmarks and funding treat taste as something you can train and sell. Snorkel's Senior SWE-Bench has a "Taste Judge" (Jun 2026), Taste-Bench is on arXiv, and Taste Labs raised an $18.5M seed from CRV and Amplify (Jun 2026) to supply taste and preference data to the labs.

H4 is not a hidden failure any more. It is the most-discussed engineering-management topic of 2026. By STATUS lesson #2 (more public pain means more competitors), that is a bad sign before we start.

## 1. Role 1: first-principles product thinker

### Extreme future (2028-2029)
One senior engineer or PM directs 50-100 agents. Code, tests, docs and migrations cost almost nothing. Each human has a fixed daily budget of high-quality judgments, about 30-80 consequential decisions. An org with 200 seniors therefore has about 10K judgment-units a day, against agent fleets that can produce 500K+ decision points a day. Throughput equals judgment supply times the share of decisions that do not need a human.

### Second- and third-order consequences
1. **Specs stop being documents and become a queue of questions.** Agents get better at asking ("ask or assume?", arXiv 2603.26233). The scarce artifact is the answer to the question an agent raises at 2 a.m., not the upfront PRD. Most of those questions repeat: the same intent comes up across tasks, repos and agents.
2. **Judgment gets spent on the wrong things.** Seniors review whatever lands in their queue, not what is most valuable. Rubber-stamping (the falling review times above) means the org pays for judgment it does not receive. Nobody measures whether a given human approval changes outcomes.
3. **"Matches intent" can only be checked after release.** Tests prove the spec was met. Only production behavior shows the intent was met. The verification boundary moves into rollouts and analytics.
4. **Third-order: judgment becomes a capital asset.** An org's accumulated accept/reject/override decisions, linked to outcomes, become its proprietary "taste model". Firms compete on how quickly a new decision reuses an old judgment. Senior headcount planning becomes judgment-capacity planning.
5. **Third-order: accountability concentrates.** One person signs off on the output of 100 agents, so the question "who approved this intent?" becomes a liability question. Round 28 showed that money there goes to the licensed signer, though.

### Candidate infrastructure layers
- **L1. Judgment Ledger and Router.** Every human intent decision (a spec answer, review verdict, override or rollback) is captured with its context and linked to its downstream outcome (revert, incident, metric). From that the system computes the value of human judgment for each decision class and how calibrated each judge is. Each pending decision is then routed one of three ways: resolved automatically from precedent, sent to the cheapest qualified judge, or escalated to a senior.
- **L2. Intent Settlement.** Executable outcome contracts. Every agent change carries a machine-checkable "intended effect" (a behavior, a metric delta, or a guardrail) that is settled against production telemetry. Spec authors and agents get an intent-to-outcome hit rate.
- **L3. Organizational intent graph and decision markets.** A versioned graph of goals, constraints and decisions that agents query. Where judgment is contested, internal forecasting markets price product bets.

## 2. Role 2: technical infrastructure architect

Where the system breaks at 100x:
- **Review as a synchronous gate does not scale.** Code review is O(PRs) human attention. At 100x PR volume it becomes rubber-stamping or a hard queue. The missing primitive is a priority scheduler for human attention, ordered by expected loss if the decision is wrong and by how uncertain the agent is. It behaves like an OS scheduler for a resource with about 50 slots a day per human.
- **Clarifications are lost.** An agent's question and the human's answer live in one chat or PR thread. There is no addressable "decision object" with a key, scope and expiry, so the same question is asked 1,000 times. The missing primitive is content-addressed precedent: a decision with a scope (repo, product area, customer tier), authority, expiry and provenance that any agent vendor can query. This would be the "OpenTelemetry for decisions" moment: one schema that Claude Code, Codex, Devin and Cursor all emit and read.
- **The outcome join does not exist.** Spec ID → PR → flag → metric → incident are held in five systems (Linear/Jira, GitHub, LaunchDarkly, Amplitude/PostHog, PagerDuty). Without that join, no one can compute "did this judgment matter?". The primitive is a decision-to-outcome trace ID that is propagated end to end.

**Architect's assessment.** L1's "decision object plus outcome join" is the most primitive-like idea. It looks like a new telemetry signal type: traces carry code execution, decision objects carry human intent. L2 is mostly a composition of feature flags, guardrail metrics and experiments, all of which exist. L3's intent graph is a knowledge-graph product; Brief, Augment's living specs and Linear already approach it.

**What could a platform team build in a sprint?** The decision log, a review risk-tiering heuristic and a flag-plus-metric gate, yes. Cross-vendor calibration of judges against outcomes is a quarter of work, not a sprint. It also needs incident and revert data that platform teams do have.

## 3. Role 3: skeptical VC partner

- **Where the money is.** Senior engineering and PM time is the most expensive line in software companies. Recovering 20% of senior judgment at a 500-engineer company is worth about $10-20M a year. That is real value, but it does not become a budget line until someone names it. Today it is spread across eng-productivity analytics (DX, Jellyfish, LinearB, Pensero), AI code review (CodeRabbit, Greptile, cubic, Graphite) and release control (LaunchDarkly, Harness).
- **Who captures value.** Value goes to whoever owns the queue where the decision sits. Specs and questions sit in GitHub Mission Control/Plan Mode and Linear's agent API. Reviews sit in GitHub, GitLab (Duo MR risk tiering), cubic and CodeRabbit. Outcomes sit in LaunchDarkly CodeControl, Harness, and Dynatrace with DevCycle. An overlay that only routes decisions sitting in other systems has weak capture, the HumanLayer lesson from round 9.
- **What would make me lead the seed.** Proof that a cross-vendor judgment dataset compounds: customers' decision precedents materially cut senior-review hours (for example, more than 40% of agent clarifications auto-resolved and more than 30% of senior reviews removed with no rise in escaped defects), plus an outcome join that no single platform can see because it spans GitHub, Linear, LaunchDarkly and PagerDuty.
- **What stops me.**
  1. Every coding-agent vendor ships memory and rules (Cursor rules and Bugbot learned rules, CodeRabbit learnings, Claude memory), and cutting clarifications through precedent is their roadmap.
  2. Review routing is a GitLab Duo work item and a cubic feature.
  3. "Judge calibration" is productivity analytics, and Pensero Calibrate already compares reviewers of AI code with reviewers of human code.
  4. The labor-ledger thesis was already killed in round 28 (Jellyfish, DX).
  5. The buyer (VP Eng) buys outcomes such as fewer incidents and faster cycle time, not "judgment allocation".

## 4. Red team: competitors (searched Jul-Oct 2026)

| Layer | Who already owns or approaches it |
|---|---|
| Spec authoring/living specs | GitHub Spec Kit (72K+ stars), AWS Kiro, Tessl (spec-as-source; also executable-spec evaluation of agent code), Augment Intent (public beta Feb 2026, PH Apr 15; coordinator plus a **verifier that checks results against the living spec**, bidirectional specs), Linear agent API (drafts specs), Productboard Spark (drafts delivery specs), Atlassian Rovo (writes acceptance criteria), BrainGrid, Tekk.coach |

| Clarifying questions | GitHub Plan Mode (asks clarifying questions before code), Mission Control (one inbox across agents), Linear |

| Judge calibration/review effectiveness | Pensero Calibrate, DX, Jellyfish, LinearB |
| Intent → production outcome | LaunchDarkly **CodeControl** ("ship AI-generated code confidently", guardrail metrics, auto-rollback), Harness continuous verification, Dynatrace + DevCycle (acquired Jan 2026), Metoro post-release verification, Statsig (acquired by OpenAI in 2025, unverified this session), Amplitude AI agents |
| Intent-based verification | Momentic ($15M Series A; intent-based test format; verified more than 80K PRs) |
| Taste/judgment models | Taste Labs ($18.5M seed, CRV/Amplify, Jun 2026; lab-facing), Snorkel Senior SWE-Bench |
| Decision markets | Pre-demand; corporate prediction markets (Google, HP) have a long history of being abandoned (Deloitte piece revives the idea; no AI-native vendor found) |

**Layer by layer:**
- **L2 (Intent Settlement): dead.** LaunchDarkly has already rebranded around AI-built code (CodeControl). Harness, Dynatrace with DevCycle and Augment's verifier cover the rest.
- **L3: dead.** The intent graph belongs to Brief, Linear, Augment and Tessl. Decision markets are pre-demand.
- **L1 is the only survivor and it is fragile.** Its parts already exist: precedent belongs to agent memory, routing to GitLab and cubic, calibration to Pensero and DX. The one new idea is the cross-vendor decision-to-outcome join used to allocate senior attention, and that is a feature of whoever owns the review queue.

## 5. Finalist write-up: Judgment Ledger and Router (best surviving thesis)

- **One-line problem:** Senior engineers and PMs are now the throughput limit on agent fleets, yet nobody knows which of their judgments change outcomes, so scarce judgment goes to rubber-stamping while the same intent questions get re-answered thousands of times.
- **Why now:** Agent PR volume has exploded. 81% of teams report more review time, review time per AI PR is falling (rubber-stamping) and incidents per PR are up about 243%. Orgs are removing humans (sign-off fell from 40% to 25%) because they cannot allocate them.
- **Exact buyer:** VP Engineering / CTO; co-signed by the Head of Platform/DevEx.
- **Exact ICP:** AI-forward B2B software companies with 150-1,500 engineers, more than 30% of PRs agent-authored, and at least two coding-agent vendors in use (Claude Code plus Codex/Cursor/Devin), on GitHub plus Linear or Jira plus LaunchDarkly plus PagerDuty.
- **Current workaround:** CODEOWNERS, ad-hoc "AI PRs need two approvals" rules, review heuristics in CodeRabbit or cubic, instruction files and rules repeated per repo, DX/Jellyfish dashboards, and seniors triaging by gut feel.
- **Why incumbents cannot easily own it (claimed):** The decision-to-outcome join spans 4-5 vendors. Agent vendors see only their own agent, GitHub has no incident or metric outcomes, and LaunchDarkly has no spec or review decisions. **Red-team rebuttal:** GitHub plus Copilot, or Atlassian plus Rovo plus Compass, can each assemble most of the join. GitLab is already building risk tiering.
- **30-day MVP:** A GitHub app plus PagerDuty and LaunchDarkly connectors. It back-fills 12 months of PRs, reviews, reverts and incidents and produces a "Judgment ROI" report: which review classes caught real defects, which approvals were rubber stamps, and which decision types recur and could be resolved from precedent. It then runs a live router that labels each new agent PR as auto-merge eligible, junior, or senior, with a calibrated escaped-defect estimate.
- **Pilot design:** Two to three design partners, 30 days. Shadow mode for two weeks, then routing for two. Success means senior review hours down 30% or more, escaped defects not up (measured by reverts and incidents within 14 days), and at least 25% of agent clarifications auto-answered from precedent.
- **Pricing hypothesis:** About $40-80 per engineer per month, or $1-3K per routed senior, framed as reclaimed senior capacity. ACV $80-400K.
- **Expansion path:** Review routing → clarification precedent service (cross-agent MCP) → judgment-capacity planning (which seniors and teams are the bottleneck) → an org-specific "taste model" licensed back into agents → beyond code (legal, finance and ops agents use the same judgment ledger).
- **Moat:** The outcome-labeled judgment dataset per customer, plus a cross-customer prior on which decision classes need humans. **Red team:** the per-customer data is easy to rebuild, and a cross-customer prior is weak because intent is org-specific.
- **Why $10B+ (bull case):** Human judgment becomes the scarce input of every AI-native organization, across code, legal, finance and ops. A neutral system that allocates and reuses judgment would be the "ERP for judgment". **Bear case:** it is a $20-50M ARR feature that ends up inside the review or agent platform.
- **Direct competitors and adjacent threats:** GitLab Duo risk tiering, cubic, CodeRabbit, Greptile, Graphite, Pensero Calibrate, DX, Jellyfish, LinearB, GitHub Mission Control/Plan Mode, Linear agent API, Brief, Augment Intent, LaunchDarkly CodeControl, Taste Labs (if it moves to enterprises), and agent-vendor memory (Cursor, Claude, Codex).
- **One sentence to send a CTO:** "Your seniors are now the bottleneck on your agents. We show you which of their reviews and answers actually change outcomes, and route only those decisions to them."
- **Hard kill criteria:** (1) The back-fill shows no meaningful difference between decision classes in how often senior review catches defects (judgment is not allocatable). (2) Design partners say GitLab Duo, cubic or CodeRabbit routing is "good enough". (3) Less than 15% of agent clarifications repeat. (4) The VP Eng will not pay above $30K without a cycle-time or incident KPI. (5) GitHub ships review routing that is aware of outcomes.

## 6. Lessons for the war room
1. H4's "public decoy" is not just Kiro or Tessl. The whole spec → review → release chain was claimed in 2026 by platforms rebranding for AI: LaunchDarkly CodeControl, GitLab risk tiering, GitHub Plan Mode, Augment Intent with its verifier.
2. "Human judgment is the bottleneck" is a consensus narrative (Addy Osmani, CodeRabbit, Snorkel, Taste Labs). Funding has gone to judgment as training data for labs (Taste Labs), not judgment allocation inside enterprises. If anything is left, it is non-code judgment allocation (legal, finance, ops agent fleets). That sits outside H4 and probably maps to the round 9 H kill (human decision layer).
3. The one primitive-shaped idea is a cross-vendor decision object with an outcome trace ID ("OpenTelemetry for intent"). It is a standard, not a company, unless an incumbent with the queue sponsors it. Worth watching for whether GitHub or Linear proposes a schema; do not build it now.
