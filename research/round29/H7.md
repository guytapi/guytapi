# Round 29 — H7: "Decisions live in agent sessions and disappear"

Date: 2026-10-06. Searches used: 23/25. Every claim is backed by a search snippet from this session (no pages were opened, because WebFetch is blocked). Anything inferred or not checked is marked (unverified) or (inference).

**Verdict: KILL.** The best thesis is the **Precedent Engine**: past reasoning compiled into constraints that agents must respect, across vendors. It scores **5.7 on average after the red team**. Two other layers were checked and score lower: the vendor-neutral reasoning archive (4.6) and decision provenance as an IP asset (5.2). No outcome-B candidate either.

The failure is real and is getting structurally worse: vendors delete transcripts and hide the model's raw reasoning. But this is the fastest-converging problem we have looked at in round 29:
- **Capture** is free and open (Entire Checkpoints, MIT; Git AI; Cursor Agent Trace spec).
- **Retention and export** is being built by the vendors themselves (GitHub Copilot session streaming, Jul 2026; Anthropic Compliance API) and absorbed by archive incumbents (Smarsh, Purview, Datadog).
- **Turning sessions into memory** is native in the agents (Codex memory, Apr 2026) and is the stated thesis of Entire ($60M seed) and Unblocked ("decision-grade context").

## 0. Evidence that the behavior is starting now

- **Transcripts are vanishing by default.** Claude Code deletes session `.jsonl` files older than 30 days at startup (`cleanupPeriodDays`). Several GitHub issues (#62476, #62959) and The Register cover users losing their history. Anthropic calls it a security default. Community tools such as RawClaw exist only to keep copies.
- **The real reasoning is not exportable at all.** OpenAI, Anthropic and Google return summarized or encrypted reasoning ("Encrypted, just not for you", Aug 2026; CSA research note, Aug 20 2026). For Claude, the full thinking output requires an enterprise agreement (snippet, unverified). So even a perfect transcript holds the model's *summary* of why, not the why itself.
- **Vendors are opening up session export, because regulated buyers demand it:**
  - GitHub Copilot agent session streaming (public preview Jul 2-3 2026) sends prompts, responses and tool calls from all Copilot clients to a SIEM or Purview. The REST pull only covers the last 48h.
  - The Anthropic Compliance API feeds Smarsh (May 26 2026), Datadog, Varonis and RunReveal.
- **Repo-linked capture exists and is free:**
  - Entire Checkpoints: launched Feb 10 2026; $60M seed at $300M (Felicis, Madrona, M12). It supports Claude Code, Gemini CLI, OpenCode, Cursor, Factory and Copilot CLI, and writes AI summaries of "intent, outcome, learnings, friction" at commit time.
  - Git AI: git-notes authorship logs linking each line to its agent and transcript.
  - Cursor Agent Trace: an RFC v0.1, adopted by Cline, OpenCode and Git AI. Thoughtworks Radar lists both Entire and Agent Trace.
- **Turning past sessions into lessons is already native or open source:**
  - Codex built-in memory (Apr 16 2026) turns completed sessions into injected memories.
  - Open-source and indie tools: cass_memory_system ("session history → confidence-tracked rules"), Harness Starter Kit ("failure memory, drift checks"), and dozens of ADR skills (affaan-m/ecc, ruflo adr-create/adr-review) that auto-detect decision moments and check PRs against ADRs.
- **Legal pressure is starting:**
  - The USPTO's revised inventorship guidance (Nov 28 2025) says only a human's *conception* counts. Agent sessions therefore become evidence of who conceived what.
  - Seyfarth (Sep 9 2026, "From Prompt to Proof") and Kilpatrick (Jul 2026) write that gen-AI logs are discoverable, and that short default retention conflicts with litigation holds.
  - EU AI Act high-risk documentation obligations apply from Aug 2026 (snippet).

Read: the hidden failure (lost "why") is real and documented. But the visible infrastructure gap (capture, retention, export) was closed between Feb and Jul 2026.

## 1. Role 1: first-principles product thinker

### Extreme future (2028-2029)
A 500-engineer company runs about 5K agent sessions a day across 4-6 vendors (coding, support, ops, data, finance). Most consequential choices are made inside those sessions, for example "retry with idempotency key", "denormalize this table" or "skip this vendor because of X". The human approves a diff or a plan, not the reasoning. Transcripts expire after 30 days locally, and vendor retention varies. The model's raw reasoning is encrypted and owned by the lab.

### Second- and third-order consequences
1. **Precedent amnesia at fleet scale.** Agent N+1 re-proposes the approach that agent N rejected after an incident. Each repeat is cheap to make but expensive when it lands. The organization cannot "learn" because the lesson never left the session.
2. **The "why" moves to the labs.** Raw reasoning tokens sit encrypted at the vendor. The company owns the code but not the thinking. Switching vendors means losing the reasoning trail. This makes reasoning data a lock-in vector, much as email archives were in the 2000s.
3. **Legal exposure flips both ways.** Patents need proof of human conception, and trade-secret cases need proof of independent development. Both now depend on agent logs that may be deleted (spoliation risk) or may contain the secret itself (leak risk).
4. **Onboarding inverts.** New hires and new agents ask the archive rather than people, so a missing archive means silent re-derivation from code. Code shows *what*, never *what was rejected*.
5. **Third-order:** decisions become dense enough to need governance of their own. Which past decision is still binding? Who can supersede it? Is precedent effectively case law for the company?

### Candidate infrastructure layers
- **L1. Reasoning Archive:** a vendor-neutral, customer-owned store of all agent sessions, with retention and legal hold, export from every vendor, and normalization into one schema.
- **L2. Decision provenance as an IP asset:** derive inventorship, conception dates and independent-development evidence from sessions, for patent counsel and trade-secret defense.
- **L3. Precedent Engine:** extract binding decisions and rejected alternatives from sessions, incidents and reverts. Compile them into machine-checkable constraints that every agent sees at plan time and is checked against at PR or action time, with supersession workflow.

## 2. Role 2: technical infrastructure architect

**Where it breaks at 100x:**
- **Volume.** About 5K sessions/day × ~200K tokens is roughly 1B tokens/day of transcript (inference). Nobody reads it, and RAG over raw transcripts returns noise: most of a session is tool output, not decisions. The useful unit is the *decision*, at about 0.1-1% of the tokens.
- **Missing schema.** Agent Trace covers *code attribution* (which lines came from which conversation). It does not cover a *decision object*: claim, alternatives, evidence, scope (which files, services or tables), binding strength, expiry, supersedes or superseded-by, and the human who accepted it.
- **Non-exportable reasoning.** Encrypted reasoning means the only durable "why" is what the agent writes out in its own words. So the primitive must be *emitted at decision time by the agent*, the way OpenTelemetry spans are emitted, not reconstructed later from transcripts.
- **Enforcement point.** A decision is useful only if it is checked where the next agent acts: plan approval, PR review, infra apply. These hooks are owned by the agent harness (CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md, memory), code review (CodeRabbit, Greptile, cubic) and GitHub.

**The missing primitive (Kafka/OTel analogy):** a *decision event*. It would be an open schema plus a small SDK: agents emit `decision.made`, `alternative.rejected` and `constraint.asserted` with scope. A store keeps these with supersession semantics, and a resolver answers "what binds this diff?". This is a real and clean primitive. But it is a spec, and specs get standardized for free: Agent Trace already exists under Cursor with Cline and OpenCode adoption, and Entire calls itself "a universal semantic reasoning layer for every agent". The value is not in the format.

**Could a platform team build it in a sprint?**
- L1: yes. Pipe GitHub streaming and the Anthropic Compliance API to S3 with object lock, and set `cleanupPeriodDays` to 3650.
- L3 v0: yes. An ADR folder, a skill that writes ADRs, and a review bot that checks diffs against them is days of work using open-source skills.
- The hard part of L3 is precision: knowing which precedent binds which diff without flooding reviewers. That is a model-quality problem the agent vendors are best placed to solve.

## 3. Role 3: skeptical VC partner

- **Where the money is:**
  - L1 is records retention. Money goes to Smarsh, Global Relay and Purview (compliance budget, per seat) and to Datadog or the SIEMs (per GB). They already ingest Claude Enterprise and Copilot sessions.
  - L2 is patent and legal spend. Money goes to law firms and to IP tools (IP Copilot, Solve Intelligence, ip.com IQ Ideas), which already scan code and communications for inventions. Round-28 lesson: money goes to the licensed signer (the patent attorney), not the record keeper.
  - L3 is engineering productivity and quality. That is a real budget, but it is contested by the agent vendors (memory is a core retention feature for them), by review bots, and by Entire (which has $60M, ex-GitHub CEO distribution, and the "why" narrative).
- **Who captures value:** the agent vendor that owns the session loop (Anthropic, OpenAI, Cursor, GitHub). Memory that makes their agent stop repeating mistakes is *their* moat, and they will not let a third party hold it. Cross-vendor neutrality matters to the CTO only once a company uses three or more agent vendors at depth, and today most standardize on one or two (inference; partly visible in GitHub streaming covering "all Copilot clients").
- **What would make me lead:** proof that precedent violations cause measurable incidents or rework, for example "20% of agent-caused incidents repeated a previously rejected approach". I would also need a design partner paying $100K+ for cross-vendor precedent enforcement that Entire, Unblocked and native memory do not deliver.
- **What stops me:**
  - Entire is the obvious Series A for this exact story.
  - Unblocked is already marketing "decision-grade context".
  - Codex and Claude memory are free.
  - ADR-review skills are free.
  - Archives are owned by Smarsh and Microsoft.
  - There is no buyer line item called "precedent".

## 5. Finalist write-up: the Precedent Engine (best surviving thesis)

- **One-line problem:** agents across vendors keep re-proposing approaches the company already rejected (after incidents, reverts or design reviews), because the rejection lived in an expired session.
- **Why now:**
  - Transcripts expire (Claude Code 30-day default).
  - Raw reasoning is encrypted by the labs.
  - Fleets are big enough that repeats are frequent.
  - USPTO and litigation pressure makes the "why" a legal asset too.
- **Exact buyer:** VP Engineering or a principal engineer who owns architecture standards; secondarily the Head of Platform/DevEx.
- **Exact ICP:** a 300-3,000-engineer AI-native SaaS company 2+ years into heavy agent use, running at least 2 coding-agent vendors plus agents for ops and infra, with a postmortem culture (written incidents and reverts).
- **Current workaround:** CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md files edited by hand after incidents; ADR folders; Entire checkpoints for search; native memory; senior engineers catching repeats in review.
- **Why incumbents cannot easily own it:** they can, which is the problem. The only defensible angle is *cross-vendor and cross-domain* precedent: code, infra, data and support agents all bound by the same decisions. No single agent vendor will enforce constraints inside a rival's agent.
- **30-day MVP:** ingest 12 months of postmortems, reverted PRs and Entire/Git AI checkpoints. Extract about 200 decision objects with scope. Expose them through an MCP server ("what binds this change?") and a PR check that flags precedent violations with a link to the original reasoning.
- **Pilot design:** replay the last 90 days of agent PRs at 2 design partners. Count how many violated an extractable prior decision, and how many of those caused rework or incidents. Then run live for 30 days.
- **Pricing hypothesis:** $40-80/engineer/year platform fee, or $50-150K/year per company (inference).
- **Expansion path:** coding agents → infra/IaC agents → data agents → support/ops agents. Then decision governance (who can supersede a decision) and IP and legal exports.
- **Moat:** the company's curated precedent graph and supersession history (switching cost), plus measured precision on "what binds this diff". The moat is weak: the extraction is model-driven and replicable, and native memory sits closer to the loop.
- **Why $10B+:** only if "company case law for agents" becomes a required control plane every agent must query before acting, like an IAM policy engine for intent. Plausible in theory, but the agent vendors and Entire stand closer to that position.
- **Competitors and adjacent threats:**
  - Entire: the main threat.
  - Unblocked.
  - Native memory in Codex, Claude Code and Cursor.
  - Augment Context Engine.
  - Review bots with learned rules (CodeRabbit, Greptile, cubic).
  - Tessl: skills and specs.
  - Free ADR-review skills.
  - Glean.
  - GitHub, which could join Copilot session streaming with code-scanning-style rules.
- **CTO sentence:** "Your agents keep re-proposing things you already decided against after an incident. We turn every past rejection into a constraint that every agent, from any vendor, checks before it acts."
- **Hard kill criteria:**
  - The replay finds fewer than 5% of agent PRs violating a prior extractable decision.
  - Entire or Unblocked ships PR-time precedent checks before the MVP.
  - Design partners say native memory plus AGENTS.md is "good enough".
  - No partner commits $50K+.

### Other layers (scored briefly)
- **L1. Reasoning Archive: 4.6.** GitHub and Anthropic provide export; Smarsh, Purview and Datadog store it; Entire's Checkpoint Remote covers code. A platform team can build it in a sprint (S3 object lock). The only unsolved part is raw reasoning, which is locked by the labs and cannot be solved by a startup.
- **L2. Decision provenance as an IP asset: 5.2.** Real legal hook: USPTO human-conception rule, discoverability of AI logs, trade-secret "reasonable measures". But IP Copilot, Solve Intelligence and ip.com already harvest inventions from code and comms. The budget is outside counsel, and the round-28 lesson applies (money goes to the signer). A weak niche remains, "independent-development evidence for clean-room and trade-secret defense", but it is too small to be venture-scale (inference).

## 6. Lessons for the war room

1. **H7's decoy was not only Glean or DeepWiki.** The real owners of this hypothesis were funded six to eight months ago: Entire (Feb 2026). The vendors shipped the export pipe in Q2-Q3 2026 (GitHub streaming, Anthropic Compliance API to Smarsh).
2. **New structural fact worth keeping:** the labs encrypt or summarize raw reasoning. Customer ownership of the true "why" is therefore impossible without lab cooperation. Any "own your reasoning" thesis depends on a lab policy change. That is a possible future regulatory wedge (EU AI Act documentation), but it is not a startup today.
3. **Memory and learning loops are a retention moat for agent vendors.** Third parties sitting between an agent and its own memory get squeezed. That repeats the round-28 lesson for coordination and change tracking.
